Reading the policy tea leaves

Unusually for this stage of the cycle, a rate hike is openly on the table — yet futures still favor a hold at the September 16 meeting.

The Federal Reserve has held the funds rate at 3.50–3.75% since its July 29 decision. Futures lean toward another hold at the September 16 meeting — CME FedWatch put the odds of no change near 59% in late August — though persistent, partly supply-driven inflation has kept an unusual hike scenario alive even as the labor market and GDP soften.

Globally, the IMF describes an economy that is steady but divergent: resilient technology investment and accommodative financial conditions have offset the drag from shifting trade policy, holding world growth near 3.3%. The risk case is a policy misstep — tightening into a slowdown — that markets watch closely with each data release.

Extend, partner, and wait

How lenders are navigating the wall

With new loans priced near 6.24% against 4.76% on expiring debt, many borrowers can't refinance economically — so lenders increasingly extend terms rather than force distressed sales. Delinquencies have stayed contained (~1.52%), but the pressure is deferred, not resolved.

Banks hold roughly 60% of maturing loans and are pulling back, while private credit firms expand into the gap — often partnering with the same banks to share risk. The MBA still sees originations climbing 27% in 2026 as capital cautiously re-engages.

Credit spreads are calm — for now

Commercial real estate is a debt business, and the spread lenders charge over the risk-free rate is the tell. Tight spreads mean capital is available and refinancing is cheaper; a widening signals risk aversion and a harder road for the maturity wall. Both investment-grade and high-yield spreads currently sit near the low end of their range.

Why spreads matter for CRE

The risk premium on top of the base rate

A CRE loan's all-in cost is the base rate (Treasury or SOFR) plus a credit spread. Even if the Fed holds rates steady, a widening in spreads raises borrowing costs and tightens the refinancing window on maturing debt.

High-yield spreads are the more sensitive gauge of risk appetite. When they blow out, lenders retreat and distressed CRE situations get repriced quickly — worth watching as an early warning alongside the bank data on the CRE tab.
